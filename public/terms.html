Terms of Use

Updated 20 September 2026

Resourcify is a planning tool. It is being tested with a few organizations. By using it you agree to these terms and to the privacy page. It is for adults 18 and over.

What the tools can and cannot do

The benefits finder shows programs that may fit. It does not decide if you qualify. It does not work out how much you would get. It does not apply for you. Only the agency that runs a program can decide.

The budget uses the numbers you type. Photo results, counts and prices can be wrong. Recycling values use rates you enter. They are not quotes. Check everything before you rely on it.

No emergency or professional service

Resourcify does not give medical, legal, money or housing advice. It cannot tell you that supplies are safe or enough. Maps and directions can be wrong or out of date. A route on a map is not a promise that it is safe. Check hours and what a place accepts before you go.

Resourcify does not call emergency services. It does not take safety reports. In an emergency in the U.S., call 911. For local help, call 211. A radio message that sends is not proof that anyone got it.

What leaves your phone

Photo analysis and location search send what you choose to outside companies. The privacy page explains when. Use those features only if that is okay with you and your organization. Those companies have their own rules.

We keep no list of users and no history. Clearing or reloading the tab erases what you typed. There is no account, no backup and no copy on our server.

How to use it fairly

Only upload photos of supplies that you are allowed to share. Keep people, documents and personal details out of them. Do not send private details over the radio. Do not try to break, flood or misuse the app. Use radio hardware the way local rules and the maker say to.

Availability

The app is offered as is. We do not promise it is always right or always up. Online features need working accounts and a connection. Offline tools only work if the app loaded once before on your phone. Radio features need the right hardware and a browser that supports it. Features may change during the pilot.

Organizations running the pilot are responsible for their own settings, accounts and how they tell people about it. They must check that Google’s rules for Gemini fit their audience before turning it on.
